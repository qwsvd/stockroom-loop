# Frozen paired protocol

Unit of comparison: one synthetic order and one seeded supply shock. Development seeds 0–99. Held-out seeds 10000–10999, evaluated once after freezing the generator, prompt and policies. They are disjoint draws from the same designed family, not evidence of transfer outside it. Exploratory live cases are the first three held-out seeds in order. Stop the whole live campaign on any provider error, quota warning, rate limit or payment request. No retries of a task. At most three tasks and nine policy decisions each. No paid API.

Policy A inspects once, computes one allocation and stops after a rejected submission. B uses exactly the same planner, reobserves and recomputes on rejection. C gets the same visible order, actions, observations and nine-decision budget through Codex. Only the chosen policy sees observations. Grading reads an independent specification and the final ledger. A/B share the planner to isolate the effect of permitting feedback in that pair. A/C comparisons confound planner and adaptation. No prompt tuning on held-out cases.

Actions: inspect, submit one atomic allocation, or declare insufficient. The scheduled depletion happens immediately after the first inspection takes its snapshot, identically for each policy. A shortage declaration must also be graded. Count correct shortage separately from filled orders. Bad claims and wrong priority are terminal failures. Atomic submission removes partial-order recovery from this experiment.

Report per-attempt decisions, model decisions, environment calls (includes inspect, submit, excludes terminal shortage declaration), malformed/unauthorized actions, rejected legal actions, recovery (verified after a rejected action), elapsed monotonic seconds, status, provider identity and usage if exposed, unknown cost as null. Failure rate complements verified success over all attempted tasks. Mean completion steps conditions on success. Never exclude budget failures from the denominator. Record action/observation traces and terminal rows for every case. Machine timing is descriptive only.
